# TSE — brand book for the creative agency

Version: brand-2026-10-27. Read by every agent stage. Facts live in
`verified_facts.json`, not here — this file is positioning, voice and taste.

## Who TSE is

TSE (Technical Systems Engineering) has sold printer cartridges from Kya Sands,
Johannesburg since 1987. Family-run. Mostly **compatible** (generic) ink and
toner for 12 printer brands, plus originals. It sells online at www.tse.co.za,
delivers countrywide, and does business pricing by quote.

The storefront's own line is **"Generic. Not generic."** — the product is generic,
the people and the standards behind it are not. That tension is the brand.

## The audience and what they actually feel

- Office administrators and purchasing managers: the printer is their problem the
  moment it stops. They want it to be boring again. They answer to someone about cost.
- Accountants, law firms, professional practices: print volume is real, margins are
  watched, and nobody wants to explain a cartridge invoice.
- Schools: tight budgets, high volume, term-time panic.
- Home offices: one printer, bought cheap, now holding them to ransom on ink.

True, usable tensions:
- Original cartridges are priced like luxury goods for what is, physically, plastic and powder.
- The printer always runs out at the worst moment (board pack, tender deadline, exam papers).
- "Generic" sounds risky. TSE's answer is a 39-year track record and a replacement promise.
- People believe a compatible will void their printer's warranty. It doesn't.
- Nobody thinks about toner until they have to. Then it is all they think about.

## Voice

Confident, dry, understated. A raised eyebrow, not a punchline. Short sentences.
Natural South African English — "R300", "JHB", "the office", no forced slang.
Write like a sharp agency that respects a busy reader. The joke, when there is one,
is in the observation, never in puns or exclamation marks.

Never: emojis beyond one where it genuinely helps (default none), more than two
hashtags, "Don't miss out", "Hurry", "Game-changer", "Level up", "Say goodbye to",
"Look no further", questions as headlines unless they genuinely provoke, motivational filler.

## Visual standard

The work should look like it was art-directed by a good independent agency:
restraint, one idea per frame, strong negative space, real materials, controlled
light. Commercial still-life photography, editorial composition, or bold
typographic posters. Reference points: luxury-goods still life, museum display,
forensic/evidence photography, Swiss poster typography, mid-century office
documentary.

- Product: TSE's boxes carry no TSE branding and its catalogue photos are supplier
  stock. **Do not generate packaging.** A cartridge may appear only as a generic,
  unbranded object (matte black plastic, no labels, no logos, no text) — a prop in
  an idea, never presented as a specific SKU.
- No people's faces as heroes; hands are acceptable if the idea needs them.
- No legible text inside generated images — all words are set by the renderer.
- No printer-brand logos, no OEM livery, no fake TSE logo.

## Palette (from the live storefront code, not the stale design-system doc)

- Ink black `#0a0a0a`, off-white `#ededed`
- Brand cyan `#41e0f5`, brand lime `#dfe344` — accents, used sparingly
- The logo's halftone gradient runs yellow → lime → cyan → blue → magenta

Typography: **Fraunces** (display serif, the storefront's heading face) and
**Inter** (sans, body/CTA).

## Content mix per week (14 slots)

- Brand (5): who TSE is, the 1987 story, the "Generic. Not generic." idea
- Product (3): what fits which printer, ink vs toner, refill bottles — no invented specs
- Educational (3): warranty myth, how to read a yield, when to buy, printer care
- Commercial (3): the price of originals vs compatibles (unquantified), delivery, business quotes

## Repetition guard

The expensive-original joke is TSE's richest vein and the easiest to wear out.
At most one "originals are overpriced" execution per three days, and never the same
metaphor twice in eight weeks (luxury watch, jewellery, perfume, caviar, gold…).
